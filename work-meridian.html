B2C Project — 2025

Connecting Riders to More Delivery Opportunities

Research showed that dispatch riders using existing apps often struggle to receive enough delivery requests, causing reduced earnings, long waiting times, and frustration. This highlighted the need for a solution that improves riders experience and earning potential.

Role
Lead product designer
Timeline
7 months
Team
1 designers, 3 engineers, 1 PM
Tools
Figma, design tokens, Photoshop

The problem

The dispatch rider industry is a critical component of the modern economy, providing efficient and timely delivery services to individuals and businesses alike. Riders using existing apps often struggled to receive an adequate number of delivery requests, which affected both their earnings and overall job satisfaction

Through research, I found that riders were frequently waiting for extended periods without receiving any delivery requests, leading to frustration, increased downtime, and reduced income. Addressing this gap was central to improving the day-to-day experience for riders and their earnings potential.

View more →

The approach

Interviews with dispatch riders revealed that many relied on a single vendor for delivery requests, leaving them without work when orders slowed. Riders also faced problems proving completed deliveries and struggled to accurately track their daily earnings.

Research showed that riders relied on scattered channels like calls, referrals, WhatsApp, and Instagram to find work, with no reliable platform dedicated to them. This led to three key goals: provide more consistent delivery requests, create digital proof of delivery, and automatically track earnings.

The interviews also surfaced something beyond the product itself — riders saw steady dispatch work as a real source of income for young people entering the gig economy, which shaped how I framed the app's value beyond just efficiency.

The outcome

To improve the dispatch rider experience, I introduced a proof-of-delivery feature that allows riders to take and submit photos of delivered items, giving them clear digital evidence of completed deliveries. 10 users tested | 53 screens designed
